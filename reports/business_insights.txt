
BUSINESS INSIGHTS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
=====================================

1. WHICH VARIABLES STRONGLY INDICATE FRAUD?
-------------------------------------------
Product category C shows the strongest fraud signal, with a fraud rate of
11.69%. Early-morning activity between 4 AM and 9 AM also shows elevated
fraud activity at 7.06%. Transaction amount shows a statistically significant
difference between fraud and legitimate transactions based on the Welch's
t-test.

2. ARE HIGH-VALUE TRANSACTIONS ALWAYS RISKY?
---------------------------------------------
No. High-value transactions are not always fraudulent. The 99th percentile
transaction amount is 1104.00, and transactions above this threshold have a
fraud rate of only 2.66%. Phase 3 also showed that the highest transaction
amounts in the dataset were legitimate transactions.

Therefore, transaction amount should be used together with other risk
signals rather than as a standalone fraud rule.

3. WHAT USER BEHAVIORS APPEAR SUSPICIOUS?
------------------------------------------
Early-morning transaction activity appears suspicious because the fraud rate
during 4 AM to 9 AM is 7.06%, which is substantially higher than the overall
dataset fraud rate of 3.50%.

High transaction frequency alone does not appear to be a strong signal.
The high-frequency group has a fraud rate of only 0.75%.

4. WHICH CUSTOMER SEGMENTS REQUIRE MONITORING?
----------------------------------------------
The following segments should receive increased monitoring:

- Transactions associated with Product Category C
- Transactions occurring during early-morning hours
- Very high-value transactions when combined with other risk indicators
- Transactions showing multiple suspicious signals at the same time

5. RECOMMENDATIONS FOR FRAUD TEAMS
----------------------------------
1. Apply enhanced monitoring to Product Category C transactions.
2. Increase risk checks for transactions occurring during early-morning
   hours.
3. Do not block transactions based only on high transaction amount.
4. Combine multiple statistical signals before escalating a transaction.
5. Use risk-based alerts to prioritize suspicious transactions for manual
   investigation.
6. In future analysis, include device-change and account-age information
   to improve fraud detection.

OVERALL BUSINESS CONCLUSION
---------------------------
The analysis indicates that fraud is associated with specific transaction
patterns rather than a single variable. Product category and time of day
show stronger practical signals, while high transaction amount alone is
not sufficient to identify fraud.

A multi-signal risk monitoring approach is therefore recommended.
